# Raw Draft's own operating philosophy

Raw Draft already has an internal approach around:

**Creation → Chaos → Clarity**

The underlying philosophy is:

Start with the opportunity.

Make assumptions explicit.

Understand the problem.

Understand alternatives.

Choose a direction.

Map constraints and failure modes.

Identify what could kill the idea.

Create bounded attempts.

Build only enough to learn.

Test.

Keep, modify or kill.

Repeat.

Do not turn this into a giant manifesto page.

Find a visual or interactive way to express cyclical creation.

Possible visual language:

DRAFT  
→ MAKE  
→ EXPOSE  
→ LEARN  
→ DECIDE  
→ REDRAFT

You may find a better representation.

The important idea is that creation is cyclical.

Builder V1 is built and live at `#/builder`. I haven't tested it yet, so it may still have bugs; a background check is running and will flag any. You're also at 91% of your usage limit, so I've stopped here.

**How it works**
1. **Start:** a large "What are you trying to figure out?" box, five example prompts you can click, and six starting cards on the board style. No login, and work is saved in your browser.
2. **Brief:** your text is turned into a structured brief: question, outcome, time, people, who's in the room, decision owner, format and evidence.
   - It uses the in-page AI, `window.claude.complete`, if available, otherwise simple keyword matching.
   - It then asks one question at a time, only for what's missing, with option cards plus a free-text notes line.
   - The brief panel on the right is clickable to edit.
3. **Workbench:** the brief on the left, the agenda in the centre and suggestions on the right. Below 1100px wide these stack into one column.

**What it does on the workbench**
- **Recommends a format:** Sprint or Workshop, with a named format and a reason why. It links to the Library page where one exists.
- **Builds the agenda:** blocks are assigned to roles (Frame, Evidence, Options, Criteria, Decide, Next test and so on), and each is filled from a real Library record. Times are scaled to fit, and breaks are added after about 100 minutes.
- **Lets you edit every block:**
  - drag it, or move it up and down
  - change its minutes
  - duplicate, lock or remove it
  - open its purpose, output, steps, what goes wrong and source, with a link back to the Library
  - Replace it with Library alternatives, each with a reason
- **Flags problems** in the agenda:
  - no decision owner
  - evidence that isn't there
  - too little time spent choosing
  - long stretches without a break
  - a block that needs something no earlier block produces
  - large groups
- **Proposes changes, applying nothing on its own.**
  - **Commands:** Fit to time, Cut 60 minutes, Make remote, Adapt for 20 people, Add a decision gate, Add evidence, Easier to facilitate, Two half-days, and Add a break.
  - Changing time, group size or format in the brief also triggers a proposal.
  - Each proposal shows what changes, why, and the minutes saved or added, with Accept and Reject buttons.
- **Stress test:** 12 checks, from Question to Follow through, each with the specific issue and a fix. There's no score.
- **Working pack:** a list of the materials this agenda needs, worked out from its structure. Only "Copy agenda" works today; the other items can't be downloaded yet.

**Entry from the Library:** every Library page now has a contextual button, such as "Build around this", "Adapt in Builder" or "Add to Builder". Library "Start here" and the footer also link to Builder.

**Not built yet:** saved projects and accounts, Run mode, a free-text "Ask Builder" box, downloadable pack files, published templates, and a separate drag-free way for touch screens.
